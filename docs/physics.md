To design a combustion engine that drives a mechanical crankshaft while consuming little to no fuel, we have to pivot away from traditional petroleum combustion and look at systems driven by alternative energy expansion, electrical ionization, or closed-loop working fluids.

In a standard internal combustion engine, fuel provides the thermal energy that causes air to expand and push the piston. To eliminate the fuel but keep the ambient oxygen, we must replace the fuel with an alternative energy source that can rapidly heat, ionize, or expand the compressed atmospheric air inside the cylinder.

Here is how you can conceptually structure this "fuel-less" combustion engine by adapting concepts of electrical plasma generation, UV photoionization, and mechanical forced induction:

1\. The Intake & Supercharging Stage
------------------------------------

Because the engine relies entirely on atmospheric air as its working fluid, maximizing the mass of air inside the cylinder is critical.

-   Forced Induction: A heavy-duty mechanical supercharger or an electric turbocharger draws ambient air into the intake manifold.
-   Pre-Conditioning: The air is compressed tightly into the cylinder, ensuring a high density of oxygen and nitrogen molecules are packed into the chamber before the power stroke cycle begins.

2\. The Photoionization & Plasma Power Stroke
---------------------------------------------

In place of a traditional fuel injector and spark plug, the cylinder head uses high-energy electrical and electromagnetic components to cause a violent expansion of the trapped air:

-   UV Photoionization: High-intensity UV-C light sources pulse into the cylinder right as the piston reaches top dead center. This UV radiation photoionizes the dense oxygen and nitrogen molecules, stripping away electrons and priming the air into a highly reactive, charged state.
-   Plasma Ignition: An intense, high-voltage electrical arc passes through the pre-ionized air. Because the UV light has already lowered the ionization threshold, the compressed air instantly collapses into a high-temperature, high-pressure thermal plasma blast.
-   The Mechanical Drive: The extreme thermal expansion of this localized plasma blast mimics a standard fuel explosion. The sudden pressure spike forces the piston down violently, driving the connecting rod and rotating the shaft.

3\. Critical Engineering Trade-offs
-----------------------------------

While this setup successfully eliminates chemical fuel dependencies, it introduces severe physical constraints that must be accounted for:

-   The Energy Balance: This is fundamentally an electrical-to-mechanical conversion engine. Creating plasma and pulsing high-intensity UV arrays requires an immense amount of electricity. The engine will not generate "free" energy; it will require a massive onboard power source (such as a high-capacity battery matrix or a generator) to sustain the electrical demands of the cylinder head.
-   Thermal and Acoustic Fatigue: Plasma arcs generate extreme heat and intense shockwaves (standing acoustic waves). Over time, this rapid thermal cycling and acoustic resonance can cause micro-fractures in standard engine blocks, requiring specialized ceramic composites or advanced cooling jackets to prevent the cylinder walls from warping.
